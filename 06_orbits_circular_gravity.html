Orbits: Gravity Supplies Centripetal Force

1. The key connection

A satellite in a circular orbit is continuously falling around the planet. The gravitational force points toward the planet's center and supplies the centripetal force.

Fg = Fc
GMm/r² = mv²/r

2. Orbital speed

Cancel satellite mass m and solve:

v = √(GM/r)

Use r = R + h if altitude is measured above the planet's surface. A satellite's mass does not affect its circular orbital speed at a given r around the same planet.

3. Orbital period

Start with v = 2πr/T and combine with v = √(GM/r):

T = 2π√(r³/GM)

For satellites orbiting the same planet, T² ∝ r³. A farther orbit has a longer period and a lower orbital speed.

4. Orbital acceleration and force

ac = v²/r = GM/r² = g(r)
Fg = mac

The gravitational field strength at the satellite's location is the satellite's centripetal acceleration in a circular orbit.

5. Frequency

f = 1/T

If a satellite completes n revolutions in elapsed time Δt, f = n/Δt, then T = 1/f.

6. Worked example: Phobos-style question

Given orbital radius r and period T:

  1. Convert T to seconds.
  2. v = 2πr/T.
  3. ac = v²/r.
  4. Fc = mac.
  5. Identify gravity as the force providing the centripetal force.
  6. f = 1/T.

7. Orbit comparison questions

  • At larger r: gravitational field and orbital acceleration decrease as 1/r².
  • At larger r: orbital speed decreases as 1/√r.
  • At larger r: period increases as r3/2.
  • Changing satellite mass alone does not change v, T, or g for a circular orbit around the same planet.

8. Elliptical orbit conceptual question

For an elliptical orbit, the net gravitational force still points from the satellite toward the planet/star. Except at special points, the force has both a component tangent to the path (changing speed) and a component perpendicular to the path (changing direction). Do not assume velocity and force are always perpendicular outside uniform circular motion.

9. Mistakes to avoid

  • Using altitude h instead of center-distance R+h.
  • Putting the orbiting satellite's mass in place of the planet's mass in v = √(GM/r).
  • Assuming gravity is zero in orbit.
  • Assuming a satellite's mass changes the gravitational field produced by the planet.
