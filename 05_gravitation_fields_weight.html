Universal Gravitation, Fields & Weight

1. Newton's law of universal gravitation

Fg = Gm₁m₂/r²

G = 6.67 × 10−11 N·m²/kg². The r in this equation is the center-to-center separation between the two masses.

  • Both masses doubled → force ×4.
  • Distance doubled → force ×1/4.
  • One mass tripled → force ×3.
  • Distance halved → force ×4.

Each object exerts a gravitational force on the other. By Newton's third law, these forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction, even if the masses are very different.

2. Gravitational field strength

g = GM/r²

Here M is the mass creating the field, and r is measured from its center. Field strength is force per unit mass, so:

Fg = mg    and    g = Fg/m

Near Earth's surface, g ≈ 9.8 N/kg = 9.8 m/s². The approximation is nearly constant because ordinary height changes are tiny compared with Earth's radius.

3. Weight versus mass

  • Mass (kg) measures inertia and does not change when an object moves to another planet.
  • Weight is gravitational force (N): W = mg. It changes when local g changes.

On a planet's surface, g = GM/R², so W = mGM/R². Compare planets using M/R²; do not compare mass alone.

4. Altitude above a planet

If an object is height h above the surface, its distance from the planet's center is:

r = R + h

Then Fg = GMm/(R+h)² and g = GM/(R+h)². Never substitute h alone for r.

5. Superposition: more than one gravitational source

Find each gravitational force separately, including its direction, then add as vectors. Along one line, opposite directions subtract and same directions add. In two dimensions, add x-components and y-components, then find the resultant.

For zero net force, the gravitational pulls must be equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. Set GM₁m/r₁² = GM₂m/r₂²; the test object's mass cancels.

6. Uniform spherical planets combined

Your scoring guide includes identical uniform planets combining. If eight identical planets of the same density merge into one sphere, total mass becomes 8M and volume becomes 8V. Since volume ∝ R³, the new radius is 2R. Surface field scales as M/R², so it becomes 8M/(2R)² = 2 times the original field.

7. Common MCQ traps from the scoring guide

  • The mass of a satellite does not change the planet's gravitational field strength at its location; the source planet's mass and center-distance determine g.
  • There is gravity in space. Astronauts in orbit are not beyond Earth's gravitational field.
  • As a satellite falls closer to a planet, gravitational force increases; the planet and satellite forces increase together and remain equal in magnitude.
  • The gravitational force direction is along the line joining the centers, toward the attracting mass.
  • A field-strength-versus-time graph near a surface may be constant when g is approximately constant.
