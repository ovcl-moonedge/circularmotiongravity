Apparent Weight, Elevators & Mixed MCQ Strategy

1. Weight and scale reading are not always the same

Actual gravitational weight is mg. A scale typically reads the normal force N, which is the apparent weight. The scale reading changes when the object accelerates vertically.

2. Elevator equations

Take upward as positive. Forces on a person: normal force N upward and gravity mg downward.

N − mg = ma
Elevator motionAccelerationScale reading
At rest / constant velocity0N = mg
Accelerating upwardUpN = m(g+a), larger
Accelerating downwardDownN = m(g−a), smaller
Free fallDown at gN = 0

Direction of velocity does not determine the scale reading; direction of acceleration does. An elevator moving upward while slowing down accelerates downward.

3. A reliable MCQ decision process

  1. Identify the system: a car, satellite, ball, person, planet, or two masses?
  2. Identify the model: uniform circular motion, vertical circle, gravitation, field, elevator, or vector superposition?
  3. Mark directions: inward radial direction, gravity direction, acceleration direction.
  4. Choose the governing principle: ΣF = ma, Fg = Gm₁m₂/r², g = GM/r², or circular kinematics.
  5. Check what is held constant: mass? speed? radius? period? source planet?
  6. Use proportional reasoning before calculating if the options are ratios.
  7. Check units and limiting cases.

5. How to review mistakes

For every missed MCQ, write one sentence completing: “I chose the wrong answer because I…” Examples: “used altitude instead of center-distance,” “treated centripetal force as a new force,” “used velocity direction instead of acceleration direction,” or “forgot the inverse-square relationship.” Then redo the problem from a blank page.

6. Final readiness checklist

  • ☐ I can distinguish velocity (tangent) from centripetal acceleration (inward).
  • ☐ I can calculate v, T, f, and ac.
  • ☐ I can draw an FBD and write ΣFin = mv²/r.
  • ☐ I can handle top, bottom, and side positions in a vertical circle.
  • ☐ I know friction and normal-force roles in turns and loops.
  • ☐ I can use Fg = Gm₁m₂/r² and g = GM/r².
  • ☐ I measure orbital distance from the planet's center.
  • ☐ I know gravity provides the centripetal force in circular orbits.
  • ☐ I can distinguish actual weight from apparent weight.
