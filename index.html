How to use your 5–6 PM private lesson

  1. Start by telling your tutor: “I understand the basic ideas, but I need to get faster at choosing equations and setting up force diagrams for MCQs.”
  2. Ask them to spend the first 5 minutes diagnosing you with one circular-motion and one gravitation question.
  3. Spend the most time on your weakest two areas. Likely high-value targets: vertical-circle radial equations; gravitational field versus gravitational force; orbital radius measured from the center; and force-vector superposition.
  4. For every worked problem, ask the tutor to pause before each equation and have you say why that equation applies.
  5. End with a 10-minute timed mini-set of 3–4 mixed MCQs. Review why each wrong option is wrong.
Do not let the lesson become an hour of watching solutions. You should be doing the drawing, choosing axes, selecting equations, and explaining the reasoning.

What to prioritize if time slips

  1. FBD + ΣFradial = mv²/r
  2. Universal gravitation and field strength (inverse-square reasoning)
  3. Gravity as centripetal force in satellite orbits
  4. Period/frequency/speed/centripetal acceleration
  5. Vertical-circle top/bottom and banked-curve setups
  6. Elevator apparent weight and remaining conceptual details

Rules for studying efficiently

  • Do not spend 30 minutes rereading one page. Read a concept, close it, reproduce the formula and explain it aloud, then solve a problem.
  • For each MCQ, predict the physics before looking at answer choices.
  • Keep an error log: concept error, diagram error, equation-selection error, algebra/unit error, or rushed reading.
  • If stuck for more than 4 minutes, mark it, look at the first step only, then restart independently.
  • Take short breaks. Do not sacrifice the whole evening to one difficult problem.
