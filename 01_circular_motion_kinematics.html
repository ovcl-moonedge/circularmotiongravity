Circular Motion: Kinematics & Relationships

1. The central idea

In uniform circular motion, an object moves around a circle at constant speed, but its velocity changes because its direction changes. A changing velocity means acceleration.

Direction rule: instantaneous velocity is tangent to the circle; centripetal acceleration points radially inward, toward the center.

2. Essential quantities

  • Radius, r: distance from the center to the object (m).
  • Period, T: time for one revolution (s).
  • Frequency, f: revolutions per second (Hz = s−1).
  • Tangential speed, v: distance traveled along the circular path per second (m/s).
  • Centripetal acceleration, ac: inward acceleration (m/s²).

3. Formula sheet

circumference = 2πr
v = 2πr / T = 2πrf
f = 1/T    and    T = 1/f
ac = v²/r
ac = 4π²r/T² = 4π²rf²

Use the version that matches the information given. If a problem gives revolutions and elapsed time, calculate f first, then T = 1/f.

4. Proportional reasoning

Change (other variables held fixed)Effect
v doublesac becomes 4 times as large
v triplesac becomes 9 times as large
r doubles at fixed vac is halved
r triples at fixed vac becomes 1/3 as large
T is halved at fixed rv doubles; ac becomes 4 times as large

For centripetal acceleration: directly proportional to v² and inversely proportional to r when v is held fixed.

5. Worked example

A cyclist travels around a track of radius 40 m, completing one revolution every 19 s.

  1. v = 2πr/T = 2π(40)/19 = 13.2 m/s.
  2. ac = v²/r = (13.2)²/40 = 4.36 m/s².

6. Common AP traps

  • Constant speed does not mean zero acceleration. Direction changes continuously.
  • Do not point velocity toward the center. Velocity is tangent.
  • Use radius, not diameter.
  • Period is seconds per revolution; frequency is revolutions per second.
  • Convert hours/days to seconds before using SI formulas.
  • When asked for a multiple of g, divide acceleration by approximately 9.8 m/s².

7. Your question routine

  1. Sketch the circle and mark its center and radius.
  2. Write down which of v, r, T, f, ac are known.
  3. Choose one equation with one unknown.
  4. Check units and whether the answer's scaling makes sense.
