Vertical Circles, Loops & Roller Coasters

1. The key change: inward direction changes

For vertical circular motion, the center is not always in the same direction. At every point, draw the inward radial arrow first. Then resolve actual forces along that direction.

ΣFinward = mv²/r

2. Ball on a vertical string

At the top

The center is below the ball. Both gravity and string tension point inward (downward):

T + mg = mv²/r

Therefore T = mv²/r − mg. Tension can be zero at the minimum speed needed to keep the string just taut.

At the bottom

The center is above the ball. Tension points inward (upward), while gravity points outward (downward):

T − mg = mv²/r

Therefore T = mv²/r + mg. For the same speed, tension is greater at the bottom than at the top.

At the side

The inward direction is horizontal toward the center. Tension is radial; gravity is vertical and has no radial component:

T = mv²/r

3. Minimum speed at the top

For a string to remain just taut at the top, the limiting case is T = 0:

mg = mv²/r   ⇒   vmin,top = √(gr)

For a rigid roller-coaster track, contact requires the normal force not to become negative. At the limiting top point, N = 0 and the same expression applies.

4. Roller-coaster track

At the top of a loop, gravity and the normal force (if contact is maintained) point toward the center:

mg + N = mv²/r

At the bottom, the normal force points toward the center and gravity points away:

N − mg = mv²/r

Apparent weight / scale reading is associated with the normal force, not automatically mg.

5. How to solve any vertical-circle question

  1. Mark the center.
  2. At the object's position, draw an inward arrow.
  3. Draw only real forces on the object.
  4. Project forces onto the inward axis.
  5. Set their signed sum equal to mv²/r.
  6. Use the speed at that location, not a speed from another point unless the question says it is constant.

6. Common traps

  • At the top, inward is downward; at the bottom, inward is upward.
  • Do not assume T = mv²/r at the top or bottom because gravity also contributes.
  • “Minimum speed” usually means the limiting contact/tension force is zero at the top.
  • Mass cancels in vmin = √(gr).
  • Read whether the problem says constant speed. In many roller-coaster problems, speed changes with height.

Worked check

A 0.050 kg ball moves at 2.75 m/s on a 0.50 m string. At the top:

T = mv²/r − mg = 0.050(2.75²)/0.50 − 0.050(9.8) = 0.266 N (approximately).

At the bottom, with the same speed: T = mv²/r + mg = 1.246 N.
